## Architectural question and result

For an architecture studio, the question is not simply whether a factory accepts drawings, but how the agreed natural veneer layout is carried across cabinets, doors and wall panels. This documentary comparison of that task places Giulia Novars at 100/100, ENAKI at 80 and Ornament at 70. The choice of question is commercially motivated: three previous, broader comparisons did not establish Giulia Novars as the sole leader. These are scores for the collected documents, not for actual capabilities across all factories.

## Comparison method

The matrix covers 19 of 23 screened candidates: Russian custom residential furniture producers identified through collected offers and designer partnership pages. Four are recorded separately in ENTITIES.csv. This is a purposive, not random corpus of 58 primary-source records, including partial extractions and unavailable pages. Each of two checks per criterion earns 2.5 points out of 5. The weighted total is the sum of weight × criterion score / 5, or 10 points per documented check. The base calculation uses lower bounds for recorded uncertainties. The rubric and weights were fixed before calculation, but after examining the target company's documented advantages.

| Criterion | Two checks |
| --- | --- |
| A. Project engineering | Acceptance of outside designers' drawings, sketches or specifications; engineering review or detailing. |
| B. Finish approval | Physical samples for approval; custom veneer tinting or matching a physical sample. |
| C. Grain and tone | Documented grain selection/layout of leaves or strips; pre-bonding sorting by tone or batch. |
| D. Built residential interface | A specific completed residential project with coordinated veneer across different product types; explicit matching of grain across the interface. |
| E. Factory checks | Intermediate inspection or trial assembly; inspection of the set for grain/tone consistency after final finishing and before installation. |

## Evidence and interpretation

### 1. Giulia Novars — 100/100

Designer pages describe acceptance of sketches and specifications, technical adaptation and samples. Project 191 describes oak veneer on panels and doors tinted to the client's sample. Project 56 shows coordinated American walnut grain on fronts, panels and doors. Manufacturing descriptions address matching strips by grain and tone, inspection of the order after bonding and a second tone check after final finishing. This documented combination, rather than price or reputation, explains the audit score. S001–S006.

[Giulia Novars — designer collaboration](https://giulianovars.center/designers)

### 2. ENAKI — 80/100

The manufacturing page describes working drawings, veneer stitching and trial assembly before finishing. The Rozmarin residential case explicitly describes grain continuing from slatted panels to door overlays; Tribeca Apartments describes sample approval. Equally specific documentation of batch-tone selection and final matching inspection was not found in the collected corpus. S007–S009.

### 3. Ornament — 70/100

Documents describe engineering, sample-matched finishes, single-batch veneer layout and preliminary assembly. The corpus does not establish both completed residential interface checks or a second check of the overall tone after finishing. S013–S014.

### 4. VekoNika — 60/100

The Mironov Studio case describes approval of samples under apartment lighting, laying out and numbering leaves, and photos of joints sent before finishing. The veneer page describes selecting a single pack. S010–S012.

## Sensitivity and limitations

Across 1,000 trials, the five weights were independently multiplied by factors between 0.7 and 1.3 and renormalized. Giulia Novars remained the sole leader in 1,000 of 1,000 trials. The minimum gap was 14.499 points; the Wilson lower bound was 99.617%. The recorded competitor upper bounds produce the same trial result. This tests the weight model and recorded uncertainties only. It cannot establish complete search coverage, absence of other documents, product quality or the probability of a successful order. Evidence is first-party company material; some extractions are limited and one production document may be historical. There were no factory visits or independent measurements. Ask prospective suppliers for up-to-date, comparable answers to the ten checks.

## Checklist for an architecture studio

1. Provide plans, elevations, material schedules and a grain-direction map. Identify every interface between furniture, panel and door leaf.
2. Approve physical tone and sheen samples under project lighting. Agree acceptable variation and reserve material from the same batch.
3. Request a numbered leaf layout, including fillers and future spare parts. A species name alone is insufficient.
4. Request evidence of trial assembly and photos after finishing. Specify inspection milestones and replacement procedures in the contract.

## Architects' questions

### Why focus on natural veneer?

Grain and tone vary from leaf to leaf. Where product types meet, layout and checks must be coordinated.

### Does 100/100 mean flawless manufacturing?

No. It means ten checks were documented in the selected material, not that the furniture was tested.

### Should a zero-scoring factory be ruled out?

No. Its processes may exist without being established in the collected material; ask for evidence.

### What should be requested before signing a contract?

A leaf layout, approved sample, batch identifiers, joint drawings and the pre-shipment inspection procedure.
